# Funded civic development and road-surface integration

The city can commission workshops, factories, warehouses, power plants, clinics and schools, in addition to existing housing/hospitality and connecting roads. Each category has a catalog entry for its real footprint, height, funding source, capacity and operating requirements. This is a demand-driven simulation with bounded choices and resident traits, not an assertion of human self-awareness or an unconstrained general intelligence.

The national review runs every six game hours, approves at most one funded project per review, checks no more than 144 candidate sites, counts existing/planned capacity and gives disaster reconstruction priority. Private industrial buildings reserve an actual resident owner's savings; generating works reserve the power utility account; clinics/schools reserve public funds. Access roads, material delivery, construction labour, inspection and remaining-escrow refunds use the existing project lifecycle. Nonresidential completion adds no apartments. Restart reconstructs actual frontage locations from durable building records.

Factory leases allow up to 50 employees each. Owners set advertised staffing targets, wages and shifts from demand, personality and available payroll reserves. Residents can accept a better offer; no population is created to fill vacancies. Commissioned workshops/factories/warehouses join the existing industrial enterprise/freight system. A factory with no output market can remain underemployed. Garment shops require private capital and real vacant premises; delivered factory garments become inventory before onsite customers can buy them. Generic enterprise accounting skips these shops and factories so simulated exports cannot fabricate their revenue.

Power plants require paid onsite operators; their staffed generation is added once to the existing grid and shares fuel/maintenance accounting. Clinics/schools require paid onsite staff and funded supplies for actual present patients/pupils. Illness remains a string/null diagnosis and treatment progress is persisted separately. Contracted facility workers are excluded from unrelated automatic recruitment and construction crews.

Map version 28 moves the four North industrial leases to the outer strip at z=-1030, connected to the existing street at z=-1000. The migration preserves lease/business IDs, cash, stock, payroll rosters, cargo and escrow, and replans affected journeys. Compact navigation bounds connect the new strip without expanding the entire North routing grid.

Viewer geometry is local: six distinct low-poly facility models, shared opaque materials, no new real lights or per-facility animation. Street surfaces use three static instanced batches with exact nonoverlapping rectangular asphalt, junction-aware sidewalks and separated surface/paint heights. Airport layers, river/harbor/sea boundaries and new construction access roads are clipped to avoid coincident surfaces. Crosswalk footprints are checked against legal asphalt. Highway signs share one canvas atlas, use #55a9a0 and white English labels, and number the 22 physical accesses clockwise.

Validation includes the full city/neural suite, unit/build checks and a local replay of a production export. The replay is read-only with respect to the live Worker: no snapshot import/reset occurs.
